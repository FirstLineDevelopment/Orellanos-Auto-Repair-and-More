## Visual Direction

- Overall visual concept: work-bench honest local repair presence with a strong garage hero, practical contrast, and red tool accents.
- Emotional impression: capable, straightforward, local, easy to contact.
- Brand personality: practical, grounded, no gimmicks.
- Existing brand signals to preserve: business name and Facebook public presence.
- Authentic asset inventory reviewed: yes
- What authentic visual material can this design be built around? At this stage, only the Facebook page identity is visible enough to guide direction. Production design uses a documented generated concept hero.
- Color strategy and rationale: warm off-white base, charcoal shop surfaces, muted red as the primary action/tool accent, amber detail color for shop-light warmth.
- Typography strategy and rationale: system sans-serif with bold, compact headings to keep the site direct and readable.
- Imagery importance: image-critical
- Photography/imagery strategy: use a generated no-logo repair-bay hero as a production-safe placeholder; replace with owner-supplied exterior/service-bay/team photos before launch.
- Hero strategy: full-bleed service-bay image with dark overlay and clear Waterbury auto-repair positioning.
- Hero authentic-image decision: no authentic production-cleared hero found; generated concept hero used and documented.
- Page composition strategy: full-bleed hero, service cards with one high-contrast feature, editorial trust section, full-width owner photo request band, form/contact conversion sections.
- Section rhythm strategy: alternate image-led hero, light services, editorial split, dark/red asset-request band, form surface, dark contact closer.
- Whitespace/depth/layering/texture strategy: garage photo depth, controlled shadows, practical surfaces, borders only where they clarify repeated items.
- CTA hierarchy: Request Service primary; Facebook page secondary.
- Signature visual element(s): compact OA concept mark, red/charcoal garage palette, service-bay hero.
- Mobile visual strategy: maintain immediate category recognition, stack proof items, keep request/Facebook actions prominent, avoid card overload.
- Brand specificity test answer: The site is specific to Orellano's by name, Waterbury signal, Facebook-first context, and documented asset needs, but it will become more truly specific after owner-supplied photos and contact details.
- Known visual compromises: generated hero is not authentic shop photography; phone/address/hours/services are intentionally unfilled until verified.
